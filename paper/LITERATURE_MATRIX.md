## How the matrix produces the research gap

Reading the **Limitation** column down the table yields four consistent
absences, which are exactly the gaps stated in §III of the paper:

1. **Rows 9–12, 15** (automation and meta-learning) all take a constructed,
   frequency-declared series as input. **Row 23** proves grain matters but
   solves a different problem (reconciliation given a hierarchy). Nothing in
   the table *chooses* a grain from an undeclared transaction table → **G1**.
2. **Rows 5, 6, 16** establish the protocol and leakage requirements
   analytically and in general, but none measures what violating them costs on
   a specific enterprise series → **G2**.
3. **Row 28** supplies combination weights but not an adoption test; no row
   requires an ensemble's reported metrics to be its own → **G3**.
4. **Rows 3, 18–22** establish how uncertainty should be constructed and
   scored, and **row 3** shows how hard it is in practice, but deployed
   business-forecasting systems typically report an unaudited confidence
   number → **G4**.
5. **Rows 24–27** solve schema and identity as standalone data-management
   problems, disconnected from any forecasting outcome. No row measures the
   forecasting cost of getting them wrong → the connective tissue this paper
   supplies.
